Documentation, READMEs and any prose written for a project have to read like a person wrote them. The dominant tell of machine-written text is not incorrect grammar, it is uniform rhythm: sentence after sentence in the 18-28 word range, every section closing on a tidy verdict. Write against that.

Rules, in order of how often they are broken:

- **At most one em dash per section.** Try a full stop, a colon or parentheses first; one of them is almost always better. This is the single most visible tell.
- **No reflexive "não é X, é Y" antithesis.** One per document at most.
- **No automatic triads.** If a list has two items, leave two. Never pad to three because it sounds better.
- **Do not end every section with a short closing verdict.** Sometimes a section just stops.
- **Bold is for technical terms the reader will search for**, never for visual rhythm.
- **Let sentence length follow meaning.** Four-word sentences are allowed. So are long ones that carry a long idea without breaking. Do not alternate short and long on a schedule, which is just a different machine rhythm.
- **First person where it is judgement or experience.** "Preferi", "descobri isso quando". Prose with nobody behind it is what reads as generated.
- **Tables only for genuinely tabular data**, two columns with a real relation between them. Otherwise prose or a list.
- **Concrete detail over general claims.** File names, numbers, what actually broke.

Never introduce deliberate errors to sound human. Sloppiness reads as sloppiness. Technical documentation also has naturally lower rhythm variance than an essay, so do not chase variance to the point of hurting clarity.
